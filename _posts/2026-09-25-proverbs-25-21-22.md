This isn't "be nice so he feels guilty." Solomon isn't telling you to fake kindness as a weapon. He's telling you: when the guy who wronged you is standing in front of you with an actual need — hungry, thirsty, stuck — you meet it. Not because he earned it. Because you're done carrying the account yourself. The coals aren't your revenge dressed up nice; they're what happens in his conscience when he gets mercy instead of the payback he expected. Your job is the bread and water. What happens in his head is between him and God. And the line "the LORD shall reward thee" is the whole reason you can afford to let it go — He's not asking you to eat the loss forever, He's asking you to hand Him the ledger.
