Most people treat giving to the poor like a loss. You hand over money or time, and it's just gone. This verse says that's backwards. If you show *chonen* — real compassion — to someone who's *dal*, worn down and empty-handed, you haven't lost anything. You've made a loan. And the borrower isn't the poor man. It's the LORD.

That's the part that should stop you. The text doesn't say God will *probably* bless you back, or that giving is just a nice spiritual habit. It says what you hand out becomes a *gemul* — a tracked debt — and God Himself *yeshallem*, pays it back in full. Not a partial refund. The same root sits behind the word shalom: whole, complete, settled. When God repays this debt, He doesn't shortchange it.

So the next time you're deciding whether to help someone who clearly can't do anything for you in return, remember you're not choosing between giving and keeping. You're choosing who holds the IOU — the poor man who can't pay, or God, who always does.
