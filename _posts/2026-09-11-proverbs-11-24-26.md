The man in this verse isn't careless with his money. He's throwing it out on purpose, and that's exactly what makes it grow. The English says \"liberal soul,\" which sounds like a personality trait. The Hebrew says something stronger: this man's whole soul *is* a blessing — pouring out on other people is just what he's built for, not a nice extra habit. That's why he \"gets watered\" back. Stop treating money and time like they vanish the second they leave your hand. Solomon says it three times in a row here: what you give away multiplies, what you clench dies, and the guy who won't let go of what other people need ends up cursed by the very people he shorted. He's not telling you to be reckless. He's telling you opening your hand is how you get fed — not how you go broke.
