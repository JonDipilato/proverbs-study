---
Solomon isn't telling you to feel sorry for people who've done you wrong. He's warning you about something sneakier: the little rush of satisfaction when life finally knocks them down a peg. That feeling seems earned. It seems fair. Solomon says God calls it evil.

Notice he covers both sizes of fall. Verse 17 starts with the big one — your enemy *naphol*, totally down. Then it narrows to the small one — he *kashol*, just stumbles, just has a bad week. Most people would say gloating over the big fall is wrong but a little "ha, serves them right" over the small stumble is fine. Solomon doesn't split that hair. Neither reaction belongs in your heart.

And the reason isn't just "be nice." It's that God is watching the whole exchange — your enemy's fall and your reaction to it — and he can be so displeased by your gloating that he turns his anger away from the person who actually did wrong and points it at you instead. The win you were enjoying can flip into your own problem. The safer move, every time, is to let their fall be between them and God, and keep your own heart out of the celebration.
