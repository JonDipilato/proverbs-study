Verse 16 is about honey, but it's not really about honey. Honey is good — nobody's being warned off it. The warning is about volume. Eat past *dayyekka*, past what's actually enough for you, and the very thing that was sweet makes you sick. That's not honey's fault. That's yours, for not knowing where the line was.

Verse 17 takes the same math and points it at people. Stay in your neighbor's house past what's good for both of you and the exact same thing happens — except now the word for "filled up" (*yisba'akha*) is describing a person who's had enough of you, not honey that's had enough of being eaten. Keep going past that and it doesn't stop at tired, it turns to hate (*usene'ekha*). Nobody plans to wear out a friendship. It happens one visit, one favor, one text too many past *dayyekka*, same as the spoon that should've stopped at the jar.

The fix isn't eating less honey or seeing people less. It's noticing where enough actually is before you blow past it — with food, with your presence, with how hard you push anybody today — and stopping there on purpose, while it's still good.
