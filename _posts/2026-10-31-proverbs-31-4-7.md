This is Lemuel's mother talking straight to her son, the king, and she isn't laying down a blanket rule about alcohol. She's protecting one specific job. A king's whole job is ruling on other people's cases — who's right, who's wrong, who gets justice. Wine and strong drink mess with exactly the part of the mind that job depends on. So she tells him flat out: don't touch it, because the moment you do, you're going to forget the law and ruin some afflicted person's case who had nobody else standing up for them.

Then verse 6 flips the whole thing around. The same strong drink that's poison for a king's judgment is medicine for somebody who's already lost everything. Give it to the man who's ready to perish, the one with a heavy heart, so he can forget his poverty and his misery for a little while. That's not a contradiction — it's two completely different situations getting two completely different answers. One man still has work that needs a clear head. The other man has nothing left to do but get through the day.

That line runs through your own life every day, too. Some of what you're facing needs you sharp — a decision that lands on somebody else, a case only you can rule on fairly. Other people you run into are just plain crushed, and they don't need your judgment, they need mercy. The mistake is mixing the two up: staying foggy when it's your turn to rule, or handing out a verdict to somebody when what they actually needed was relief.
