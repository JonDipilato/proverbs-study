These two verses aren't about two different kinds of men — they're about the same choice, made twice. Verse 15 is the choice about information. A simple man hears something and just takes it, no questions, no checking. A prudent man slows down and looks at his actual step — not the story in his head, the real ground under his feet. That's not suspicion for its own sake. It's refusing to build a day's decisions on something he never bothered to confirm.

Verse 16 is the same choice about anger. A wise man keeps a working fear — enough caution to see trouble coming and walk the other way before it grabs him. The fool drops that fear and goes straight to rage, and here's the ugly part: he feels completely confident the whole time he's losing it. He's not shaken by his own anger. He's sure of himself in it. That's what makes rage so dangerous — it doesn't feel like losing control, it feels like being right.

Put them together and the pattern's obvious. The simple man and the angry fool both skipped the same step: checking what's actually true before they acted on it. One skipped it with a rumor. The other skipped it with his own feelings. Neither one looked first.

So today, look first. Look before you pass something along. Look before you let yourself get hot. The guy who checks his step and keeps his fear isn't weaker than the confident fool — he's the only one of the two who's actually standing on something real.
