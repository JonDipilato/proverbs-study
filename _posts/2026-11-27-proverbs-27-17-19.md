Nobody sharpens a knife with a pillow. You need something just as hard pressing back — that's the whole point of verse 17. A friend who only ever agrees with you and never pushes back isn't making you better. He's just keeping you comfortable while you stay dull. The friend who tells you the hard truth, who argues the point, who calls out your bad call — that's the iron actually doing its job on you.

Verse 18 is a different picture but the same idea underneath. The guy who shows up and tends the fig tree, day after boring day, is the one who gets to eat the fruit. The guy who actually serves his boss well, even on the days nobody's watching, is the one who ends up honored. Honor doesn't get handed out for showing up once. It's built slow, out of the work nobody claps for.

Then verse 19 ties both pictures together. Look into still water and your own face looks right back at you — you can't stop it, and you can't fake it. That's what people are to each other. Your heart shows up in how you actually treat people, and it shows back up in them. Sooner or later, whatever's really in you comes out where everybody can see it.

So let the people who push back actually sharpen you instead of walking away from them. Keep doing the boring, faithful work even when nobody's watching. And quit pretending your heart is something it isn't — it's going to show up on your face either way.
