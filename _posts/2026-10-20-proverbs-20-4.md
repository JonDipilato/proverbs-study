The sluggard in this verse isn't lazy because he's weak. He's lazy because he's found a reason that sounds reasonable. It's cold out. Nobody plows in the cold. Except the cold wasn't an emergency — it was the normal plowing season, the exact window every other farmer used to get seed in the ground. He didn't miss his chance. He talked himself out of it.

That's the trap with *yacharosh*. Refusing to work isn't nothing — it's a choice made just as deliberately as any scheme, except this one is aimed at himself. He spent the planning season planning his excuse instead of his crop.

Harvest doesn't care what reason you had. It just shows up, on schedule, and asks everybody the same question: what did you plant? The guy who worked through the cold answers with a full barn. The sluggard answers by *sha'al* — the same word you'd use asking God for help, now reduced to begging a neighbor for bread, because he spent the only season he had on an excuse instead of a field.

The test today isn't whether conditions are good. They're not always going to be good. The test is whether you work anyway, now, while it still counts — or whether you wait for a better season that was never actually coming.
