Proverbs 28:22 isn't talking about somebody who works hard and gets ahead honestly. It's talking about somebody in a panic. The Hebrew word is *nivhal*, and it's the same word you'd use for a man running from a fire. That's the picture here. He's not calmly building anything. He's scared, and he's running — straight at money.

That panic turns into an evil eye. People stop being people and start being opportunities. Deals start looking like traps he's willing to spring on somebody else if it gets him ahead. The eye goes bad because the heart already went bad with hurry.

And here's the part he misses: he doesn't "consider" what's coming, because he's moving too fast to think straight. The word for the poverty headed his way is so rare it only shows up one other place in the whole Bible — this isn't "things are a little tight." This is the bottom. And it doesn't creep up on him slow. It comes, the same plain word the Bible uses for somebody walking up and knocking on a door. Whatever you're rushing toward right now, slow down and look at it straight — or it's going to walk right up and knock.
