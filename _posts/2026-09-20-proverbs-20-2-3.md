word_note: "*Naham* isn't the big dramatic roar you'd hear on a nature show — it's the growl. The warning sound. The one that tells you the attack is about to happen, right now, if you don't back off. That's the picture: a king's (or a boss's, or an angry person's) anger isn't sudden, there's a growl first. Poke it anyway and you lose — the verse says plainly, you \"sin against your own soul.\" You did that to yourself. *Riyb* is a real complaint — something you actually have a case for. This isn't \"let stuff slide that doesn't matter.\" It's: even when you've got a legitimate grievance, walking away from pressing it is what an honorable man does. These two verses are one lesson: anger and arguments cost you more than they get you. Verse 2 says don't provoke someone who's already worked up — you'll lose, and it'll be your own fault. Verse 3 says the flip side: even when you've got a real grievance, dropping it is the honorable move, not the weak one. Only fools keep poking. You don't have to win every argument to be right — you just have to stop feeding it.
