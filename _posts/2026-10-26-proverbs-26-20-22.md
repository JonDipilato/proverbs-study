---
Verse 20 pictures strife as something physical: a fire that needs fuel. No wood, no fire — that part's obvious. What's not obvious is that the "wood" for a quarrel between two people is usually a third person carrying stories between them. Take the talebearer out of the middle, and the strife doesn't need to be settled or won. It just runs out of fuel and goes quiet on its own.

Verse 21 makes it worse: some people aren't just occasional fuel, they're built to burn. "As coals are to burning coals, and wood to fire" — that's not a man who gets dragged into conflict once in a while, that's a man whose whole pattern is kindling it. He doesn't need a good reason to stir something up. Kindling strife is just what he does, the way wood just burns when it's put near fire.

Verse 22 explains why gossip works on people even when they know better: it doesn't stay on the surface. The words of a talebearer go down into the innermost parts of the belly — they get swallowed, digested, believed at a level past where your better judgment lives. That's why a story you heard at 9am can still be shaping how you treat somebody at 5pm, even after you've had time to think better of it.

None of this requires you to fix anybody else. It just requires you to stop being wood. Don't carry the story, don't add the comment, don't swallow what somebody else is trying to feed you about a person who isn't in the room. Cut the supply line and most fires you're standing next to will go out whether or not you ever "resolve" anything.
