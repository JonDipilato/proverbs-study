Most people treat "pay attention" like a one-time switch: you heard it, box checked, move on. This passage says that's not how it works. Verse 20 is the easy part — attend, incline your ear, actually listen instead of letting words bounce off you. But verse 21 is the part everybody skips: keep watching. The same word used for a path going crooked describes what wisdom does the second you stop guarding it — it doesn't announce its exit, it just drifts.

So the instruction isn't "listen once." It's "stand guard." Put these words in front of your eyes on purpose, and keep them in the middle of your heart — not the feelings part of you, the deciding part, the part that actually runs your day. That's work. It's the difference between hearing a sermon and still doing the same dumb thing by Tuesday.

And verse 22 is why it's worth the work. This isn't vague spiritual benefit — it's life and health, for your actual body. God is saying neglect isn't neutral. What you stop watching doesn't just fade from memory, it stops doing you any good. What you guard keeps working on you, body and all.
