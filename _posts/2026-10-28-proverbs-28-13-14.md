Verse 13 is a flat statement, not a suggestion: covering your sin doesn't work. Not "might not work" — it just doesn't. Whatever you're hiding keeps costing you underneath the surface even while it looks managed on top. The only road to mercy runs through confession, not around it. That's a hard trade for most people, because confessing feels like it should make things worse, not better. But the verse says the opposite: the hiding is what keeps you stuck, and the admitting is what finally lets something new happen.

Verse 14 explains why some people can do that and some people can't. It comes down to whether you've kept a living sense of God in the room with you. The man who stays alert to God — not scared stiff, but aware, the way you're aware of a boss watching or a camera running — notices his own sin quickly, while it's still small and nameable. The man who's let his heart go stiff doesn't notice anymore. He's not more guilty than the first man. He's just gone numb to it, which is worse, because numb people don't confess — they just keep stacking up trouble they can't feel coming.

Put the two together and you get a maintenance plan, not a one-time fix. Staying soft toward God is what keeps you able to say "I was wrong" the same day you do wrong, instead of three years later after the damage has compounded. Confession isn't a special event for when things get bad. It's just what a soft heart does automatically, every single day it's needed.
