This passage lays out a trade you can't dodge. You either get wise for yourself, or you scorn it for yourself. There's no third option where somebody else absorbs the cost.

Verse 10 says the fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom. Not a nice add-on once you already know a lot. The beginning. You can memorize every proverb in this book and still not be wise if you don't take God dead serious first. That fear isn't about being scared of a bully. It's about knowing He's real, His word is real, and acting like it in the small stuff — what you say, what you charge, what you do when nobody's watching.

Verse 11 says that fear pays off in multiplied days and increased years. That's not a magic trick. A man who actually fears God tends to avoid the stupid stuff that shortens his own life — the affair, the shady deal, the drunk drive home, the grudge that eats him alive. Taking God serious keeps you out of a lot of self-made disasters.

Then verse 12 lands the hammer. If you're wise, you're wise for yourself. If you scorn it, you alone bear it. Nobody splits that bill with you. Your kids don't pay it. Your boss doesn't pay it. You do. That should change how you treat every decision today — not as something you can shrug off onto circumstances or other people, but as weight that's landing on you no matter what.

So don't wait to see if wisdom "pays off" before you commit to it. Start taking God serious today, in the actual decision in front of you, because you're the one who's going to carry whatever you choose.
