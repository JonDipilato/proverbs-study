Most people don't think of themselves as foolish for answering fast — they think of it as sharp, decisive, on top of things. This verse says otherwise. The problem isn't the answer itself. It's the order. Solomon pictures a *davar*, a matter, still being laid out, and a *meshiv* who starts responding before the *yishma* — the actual hearing — is finished. He's not reacting to what was said. He's reacting to what he assumed was coming, or to the first half of a sentence he never let finish. That's *ivvelet*. Not a minor slip, a fool's move, because it skips the one step that would've made the answer worth anything. And it doesn't stay quiet. *Kelimmah* shows up next — the shame of being visibly wrong, in front of the person you cut off, because your answer landed on a matter that wasn't the one actually in front of you. The cure here isn't holding your tongue forever. It's holding it long enough to actually hear the thing before you open your mouth about it.
