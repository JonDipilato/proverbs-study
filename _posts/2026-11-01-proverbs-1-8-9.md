This is a father talking to his son, man to man, before the son even walks out the door. He isn't laying down a hundred rules. He's asking for two things: listen to the discipline I'm handing you, and don't walk off from what your mother is teaching you either. Both parents, one voice between them, one simple ask — pay attention, and don't quit on it.

Verse 9 tells you why it's worth it. Keep that instruction and it's not a cage, it's jewelry. A wreath on your head, a chain around your neck — back then, that's what you wore if you'd made it, if people respected you. The deal is simple: the correction you hate hearing right now is the same thing that makes you a man worth respecting later. Walk away from it and you don't just lose some rules, you lose the crown that comes with keeping them.

Most guys treat the voice of their parents, or whoever actually raised them right, like background noise they outgrew years ago. This verse says flip that around. The stuff you've been rolling your eyes at since you were a teenager is still worth more than you think. You don't have to like how it sounds coming at you. You just can't afford to drop it.
