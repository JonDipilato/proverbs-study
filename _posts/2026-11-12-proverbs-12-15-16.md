Verse 15 isn't about a dumb guy — it's about a stubborn one. The fool here thinks his way is right simply because it's his way. He's not asking "am I actually right?" He already decided that before anyone opened their mouth. The wise man does something that feels weak but isn't: he lets somebody else's counsel actually land on him before he reacts to it.

Verse 16 shows where that stubbornness ends up. The fool's anger is "presently known" — it shows up on his face, in his voice, in his texts, same day, no delay. There's no gap between what he feels and what he shows. The prudent man feels the same spike of anger, probably more than once a day, but he covers the shame instead of airing it in front of everybody.

Here's the connection: a guy who won't take advice is usually the same guy who can't hold his temper, because both come from the same place — treating your first reaction as automatically correct. The fix for both is the same pause. Before you answer back, before you fire off the text, before you say the thing — stop and ask whether your gut is actually right here, or just reacting.

You don't need to be a pushover to do this. Staying quiet for ten seconds before you respond isn't losing. It's the only thing that actually wins, because the guy who blows up today is still cleaning up the mess tomorrow, and the guy who held it in is already past it.
