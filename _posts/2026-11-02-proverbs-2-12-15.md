This part of Proverbs 2 isn't warning you about some obvious villain. It's warning you about the guy who sounds fine. Verse 12 calls him "the man that speaketh froward things" — someone whose talk has been twisted on purpose, built to sound good enough to follow. Verse 13 says people like that didn't stumble into it. They left the straight paths on purpose and walked into the dark. Verse 14 goes further: they're not even uneasy about it, they enjoy it. And verse 15 names the shape of it plainly — their ways are crooked, bent out of true, and so is everything that comes out of their mouth.

Here's the part that actually matters for you today: this isn't a lecture about avoiding criminals. It's about learning to notice when somebody's words and their actual direction in life stop matching. That shows up at work when a guy's story keeps shifting to fit whatever gets him off the hook. It shows up in friendships when someone talks one way and lives another. It shows up with family when the explanation sounds smooth but something underneath doesn't sit right.

Wisdom doesn't promise you'll never run into crooked people. It promises you can learn to spot the gap between what someone says and where they're actually walking — and refuse to follow either one once you see it. And it starts by making sure your own talk and your own road match first.
