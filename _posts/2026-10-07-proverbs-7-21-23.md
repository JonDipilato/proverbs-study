word_note: "*Cheleq* isn't a lie you can point to. It's smoothness — words with no friction, no snag, nothing that makes you stop and check. The verse doesn't say she threatened him or tricked him with facts. She just talked smooth long enough that he stopped checking. That's the tell: the more frictionless a pitch feels, the more you should be checking it, not less. The ox in this picture isn't scared. It's walking normally, maybe even calm, because it has no idea where the path ends. *Tebach* is a specific, violent word — this is the actual kill floor, not a metaphorical close call. The warning isn't \"be careful,\" it's \"you will not see this coming, so stop walking before you're close enough that it doesn't matter whether you see it.\""

Stop grading temptation by how it feels in the moment and start grading it by where the road goes. Smooth talk is not proof something is safe — in this passage it's the exact mechanism of the trap. The ox in verse 22 isn't struggling; it's walking calmly toward its own death because nothing along the way told it to stop. God is asking you to be the one who checks the destination before you take one more smooth, easy step.
