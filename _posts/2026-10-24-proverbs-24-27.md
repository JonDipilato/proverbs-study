It's easy to read this verse as advice for farmers and skip it. It isn't. It's advice for anybody tempted to build the comfortable part of life before the part that pays for it is actually running.

The verse doesn't say don't build the house. It says get the field work locked down first — *hakhen*, make it solid, don't half-start it — and only afterward, *uvanita beitekha*, build your house. The field comes first because the field is what feeds the house. Skip that order and you're not building anything, you're just borrowing against a harvest that hasn't come in yet.

Everybody's got a version of this. The guy who upgrades his truck before his business is actually bringing in steady work. The couple who buys the bigger place before either paycheck is secure. The side project that gets all the energy while the job that actually pays the bills gets the leftovers. None of that is wicked. It's just backwards. Today's the day to check which one you're actually feeding — the field, or the house you haven't earned yet.
