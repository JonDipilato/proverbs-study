Nobody reads this verse and thinks Solomon hates naps. He's not saying sleep is sin. He's saying watch what you *love*. The moment sleep becomes the thing you'd rather have than anything else — the thing you protect over your job, your family, your word — it starts costing you. Not all at once. A late start here, a missed call there, a job half-finished because you hit snooze one too many times. That's how *tivvaresh* works. It's the same word used for an army taking over somebody's land, and it fits, because that's exactly how poverty creeps in on a man who loves his bed too much — not as a raid, but as an occupation, one comfortable morning at a time.

The fix Solomon gives isn't complicated. *Paqach* — open your eyes. It's a verb you do, not something that happens to you while you're still half asleep. Nobody drifts into getting up early and getting things done. You force it. And the payoff isn't abstract either — *saba*, full, satisfied, with real *lechem* on the table. The verse ties getting up directly to getting fed. Open your eyes on purpose today, and don't be surprised when the day actually gives you something back.
