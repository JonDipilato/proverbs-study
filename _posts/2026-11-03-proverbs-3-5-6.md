This is one of those verses everybody says they believe and almost nobody actually does. Verse 5 doesn't tell you to feel confident about God. It tells you where to stop putting your weight: off your own *binah*, the smart plan you talked yourself into with your own head. In its place you're told to *batach* — lean your real weight on God the way you'd lean your body on a rail, not just agree with the idea that he's trustworthy.

Verse 6 takes that out of the realm of big, dramatic decisions and puts it on every ordinary *derekh* you walk today. It's not one huge leap of faith at a crossroads. It's acknowledging him — *da'ehu*-ing him — inside the small stuff: what you say back to your kid, how you handle the bill, whether you cut the corner nobody would ever notice. And the promise attached to that isn't something you manufacture by trying harder. *Yeyasher* — he will make the road straight. That part is on him, not you.

So the whole passage is really just two jobs, split down the middle. Your job: stop leaning on your own figuring, and actually bring God into the ordinary choices today, not just the big ones. His job: straighten what's crooked. Most people flip that around — they try to do the straightening themselves and leave God out of the small decisions. This verse says do it the other way.
