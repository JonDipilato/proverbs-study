Most people think of words as weightless — you say them, they're gone. Solomon says the opposite: your mouth is a field, and whatever it grows, you're the one who eats it. That's what "fruit of his mouth" means in verse 20 — not a nice turn of phrase, but an actual harvest coming back on the man who grew it.

Verse 21 pushes it further. The *lashon* — the tongue itself — holds *maveth* and *chayyim*, death and life, like two switches on the same machine. Same mouth. Same day. You've seen it: one sentence that ends a friendship, one sentence that talks a guy off the edge of quitting. The tongue doesn't pick a side for you — you do, sentence by sentence.

Then comes the warning that should actually sting: "they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof." Not "those who use it," but those who *love* it — people who love talking, love being right, love the sound of their own voice more than they love whoever's standing in front of them. That's the trap. You don't have to be a liar or a gossip to grow a bad crop. You just have to stop paying attention to what's coming out of your mouth, because you're too busy enjoying that it's coming out at all.
