word_note: "Put together, *erek appayim* literally means \"long of nose\" — a nose that takes a long time to flare. It's the exact phrase used over and over for God Himself: slow to anger. Solomon isn't asking you to never get mad. He's asking you to run a long fuse instead of a hair trigger, because the guy who blows up fast is the one who ends up looking like a fool, not the one who stayed quiet. *Qin'ah* isn't a passing \"must be nice\" thought — it's the word for a burning, possessive jealousy that eats at you from the inside. Solomon says a calm heart keeps your body alive, but this kind of envy rots your bones. He's not being poetic. He's telling you envy is a physical drain, not just a feeling you can shrug off."

Your temper and your envy aren't just "personality." Solomon says they show up in your body. A short fuse makes you look stupid in the moment — a long fuse, God's own kind of patience, makes you look wise. And chewing on what someone else has instead of being glad for your own lane doesn't just sour your mood — it wears you down for real. Cool the fuse, kill the comparison, and you keep more than your reputation — you keep your health.
