word_note: "*Abad* is the word for a servant's work — showing up and putting your hands in the dirt, day after day. Solomon isn't talking about luck. He's talking about the guy who does the unglamorous, repeated work on what's actually his, versus the guy chasing \"vain persons\" — empty men with empty schemes. One produces bread. The other produces poverty, guaranteed. *Emunah* means firmness — it's the same root behind \"amen,\" something solid you can stand on. The verse doesn't say \"a lucky man\" or \"a smart man\" abounds with blessings. It says a firm, steady, keeps-his-word man does. And it puts \"maketh haste to be rich\" directly against that steadiness — hurry and integrity don't share a lane."

Stop looking for the shortcut. Work the actual ground in front of you — your actual job, your actual land, your actual responsibilities — instead of chasing whatever get-rich scheme or empty promise just walked by. Steadiness, not speed, is what God calls faithful. The man in a hurry to get rich is, by definition, cutting a corner somewhere, and this verse says he won't get away clean.
