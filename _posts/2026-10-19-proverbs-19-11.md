Most guys think a quick temper means they're not a pushover — that firing back fast proves they're not weak. This verse says the opposite. It's not your fists or your mouth that proves you're strong, it's your *sekel* — your sense, your judgment — doing the quiet work of stretching out the fuse before your anger even gets lit. That takes more control than blowing up ever does, because blowing up is easy. Anybody can do that.

Then Solomon makes the harder point. Passing over a wrong — and he's not talking about a minor annoyance, *pesha* is a real offense, something that actually deserves a response — isn't you backing down. It's your *tiph'arah*, your glory, the thing that actually makes you look good to the people watching. Nobody remembers the guy who kept his mouth shut about a small thing. People remember the guy who had every right to blow up over something real and didn't.

That's the test today. Not whether you can avoid getting provoked — you will get provoked — but whether you've got the sense to let the fuse run long instead of short when it counts.
