---
These two verses are a matched pair, and they're both correcting the same mistake: judging a day, a job, or a meal by its size instead of what's actually inside it.

Verse 16 takes on money. Great treasure sounds like it settles everything. But Solomon adds the fine print — *mehumah*, trouble, often comes stapled to it. Money chased the wrong way, held the wrong way, or fought over, brings its own storm along with the cash. A smaller amount held with the fear of the Lord — clean, calm, honestly gotten — outweighs a bigger pile that costs you your peace to keep.

Verse 17 takes on food, but it's really about the same thing in a different room. A stalled ox is a rich man's dinner — meat, plenty, the good stuff. But if hatred is sitting at that table, none of it matters. A bowl of vegetables where love is present beats it, no contest. The meal was never really the point. Who's at the table, and what's between you, is.

Put together, these two verses are asking you one question all day: are you measuring your life by the size of the pile, or by what's actually living inside it? A raise that costs you your family's peace isn't a win. A rich dinner eaten across the table from someone you resent isn't a feast. Little and peaceful, plain and loving — that's the better math, every time.
