Solomon isn't talking about taste buds. He's talking about how sin always pitches itself: smooth, sweet, flattering, easy to swallow. Nobody gets hooked by something that announces "this will wreck your life." It gets in on honey and oil — words that feel good, attention that feels good, a deal that sounds too easy. The bitter end (wormwood, a two-edged sword, death, hell) is real, but it's downstream, and by the time you taste it you've already walked the path. Verse 6 names the actual trap: her ways keep moving, so you can never quite pin down where this is headed until you're already there. The fix isn't willpower in the moment — it's deciding now, before the honey's on your tongue, that sweet talk is not proof of anything except that someone wants something from you.
