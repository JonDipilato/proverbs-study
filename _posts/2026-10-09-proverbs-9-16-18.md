Folly sits at her own door and shouts the exact same pitch Wisdom used back in verse 4 — word for word, "whoso is simple, let him turn in hither." Same bait, different house. Her whole sales pitch for sin is one sentence: it tastes better because it's stolen and secret. Not because the water is better water. Because you're not supposed to have it. That's the entire thrill, and it's a promise, not a fact — the verb is "will be sweet," future tense, sold before it's tasted. The guy who walks through that door thinks he's walking into a party. He's walking into a morgue and doesn't know it yet (v.18). Nobody tells him the bill until after he's already swallowed.
