---
Verse 5 sets up a trade most people would never take: an open rebuke beats a hidden love. That sounds backwards until you notice the word "secret." A love that never says anything, that stays locked up where the other person can't see it, isn't doing them any good at all. A *tokhachath* — an honest case laid out where you can see it — is love that actually shows its work. It costs the other person something to say it to your face. That cost is the proof it's real.

Verse 6 makes the same point with a sharper picture. A friend's wounds are *ne'emanim* — faithful, trustworthy, the kind of word used for something you can lean your weight on. The pain is real, but it's reliable pain: it's pointing you somewhere true. An enemy's kisses, by contrast, are *na'tharoth* — not necessarily a lie so much as affection piled on way past what's needed. Too smooth, too much, too easy. The enemy isn't trying to hurt you; he's trying to keep you comfortable so you never see what's actually going on.

Put the two verses together and you get a simple test for the people around you: who's willing to cost you something to tell you the truth, and who only ever tells you what feels good? The first one is worth keeping close even when it stings. The second one is worth being careful around, no matter how nice it feels in the moment.
