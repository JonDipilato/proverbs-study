Most of us do this backwards. The second we're mad at someone, we don't go to them first — we go to everyone else. We vent to a friend, drop hints online, tell our spouse the whole story before the other person even knows there's a problem. Solomon says that's exactly the wrong order.

Verse 8 slows you down before you even pick the fight — don't rush to strive, because you don't know how it ends. Verse 9 tells you where the fight actually belongs: with your neighbor, not with the room. Verse 10 is the part that should scare you straight — the moment you drag in an extra listener, you've handed them a story they can use against you. The humiliation doesn't come from having the disagreement. It comes from how many people found out about it.

This isn't about burying conflict or being a doormat. Go have the hard conversation — Solomon wants that, not avoidance. He just wants it to stay between the two people who actually have the problem, instead of turning into gossip that outlives the argument itself.
