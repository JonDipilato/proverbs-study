The lion in verse 13 isn't real. There's no lion in the street — it's a made-up reason that sounds responsible ("it's not safe," "not the right time," "I need more info") so nobody, including him, has to call it what it is: he just doesn't want to go. Verse 15 is the killer detail — he won't even finish feeding himself. And verse 16 is the trap that keeps the whole thing running: he's more confident in his own logic than seven wise men combined. That's why the sluggard never fixes it — you can't argue a man out of a position he's already sure is smart.
