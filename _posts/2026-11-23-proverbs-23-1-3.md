Solomon isn't writing an etiquette guide for state dinners. He's warning you about a specific trap: the moment someone with power over you — a boss, a client, anyone who can make your life easier or harder — sets something good in front of you. That's precisely the moment to think hardest, not relax.

Verse 1 says consider diligently, use your mind, look at what's actually there. Verse 2 is almost violent about it — put a knife to your own throat if you're a man given to appetite. That's not about dying at dinner. It's Solomon saying your appetite is dangerous enough to need a threat that sharp. And verse 3 tells you why: those dainties, that free meal, that generous offer — it's *kezavim*. Lies. It looks like kindness. It's actually leverage.

This isn't only about food. It's about any time a powerful person's generosity shows up right when your guard is down. The fix Solomon gives isn't "never eat with powerful people." It's: know what you're there for, keep your head while your appetite is being fed, and don't mistake a full plate for a clean deal.
