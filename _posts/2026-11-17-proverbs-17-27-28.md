Most people think looking smart means having something sharp to say. This verse says the opposite: the sharpest move is often saying nothing at all.

Verse 27 isn't about a guy who's naturally quiet. The man here has *da'ath*, real knowledge, and *tebunah*, the kind of understanding that reads a room before deciding whether to speak. His silence, *choshek*, is a choice made from strength, not a personality trait.

Then verse 28 twists the knife: even a fool who *macharish* — stays quiet — and *otem* — clamps his mouth shut — gets mistaken for wise. Nobody can tell the difference from the outside. A closed mouth looks the same whether it's backed by real understanding or by nothing at all.

That should bother you a little. It means the discipline matters more than being right. You can be the smartest person in the room and blow it by talking too much, or you can hold your tongue on something you don't fully understand and walk away looking sharper than you are. Either way, the lesson's the same: count the cost of a sentence before you let it out of your mouth.
