It's easy to read "don't be a drunkard or a glutton" as a warning aimed at somebody else — some guy who can't control himself at the bar or the buffet. But look at where the passage actually starts: guide your own heart. This isn't about those people. It's about you, today, deciding what gets to run the show.

The Hebrew doesn't let the glutton off easy either. *Zolel* isn't just "eats too much" — it's tied to a word for being loose, shaking, worthless, like something with no backbone holding it together. Overindulging here isn't framed as a harmless comfort. It's framed as a loose grip on your own life, the same loose grip whether it's the bottle, the plate, the shopping cart, or the phone.

And the ending is the part that should actually get your attention: *yivvaresh*, dispossessed, and *numah*, a word used exactly once in the whole Bible, describing the kind of sluggish laziness that quietly becomes your permanent clothing. Nobody wakes up one day already in rags. They get there the way everybody gets anywhere — one small surrender at a time, until the small surrenders are just who they are. Today's the day to grab the wheel back.
