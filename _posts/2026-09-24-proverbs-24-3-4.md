Most guys build their life on whatever feels urgent that day — grab the next job, spend the next check, react to the next fire. Solomon says that's backwards. A house that stands gets built in order: plan first (wisdom), lock it in (understanding), then fill it (knowledge). Skip the planning and you can still put up walls, but the rooms stay empty — no savings, no margin, nothing put back for later. This isn't about being smarter than everybody else. It's about actually stopping to think before you build anything — a schedule, a budget, a marriage, a business — instead of just winging it and hoping the rooms fill themselves.
