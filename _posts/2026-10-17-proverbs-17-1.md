Most people would take the full table. A feast is a feast — more food, more to offer, more to be proud of. But Solomon says no, run the numbers again. Put a live quarrel next to that feast and the math flips. The *riv* costs more than the *zivchei* buys. Everyone at that table is eating well and miserable at the same time, and the misery doesn't go away because the food is good. Meanwhile the house with one dry scrap of bread and actual *shalvah* — nobody keeping score, nobody waiting to snap — is living richer than the house with the catering. This isn't a verse against nice things. It's a verse against using nice things to dodge the actual problem. If there's a fight going on in your house, buying your way around it with a better dinner or a bigger gift just delays the bill. Fix the *riv*. The quiet is worth more than the spread.
