Most people facing an enemy go straight to strategy — how do I outmaneuver this person, win the argument, get the upper hand. This verse skips all of that and points somewhere else entirely: your own walk with God.

Solomon isn't talking about a single clever move. *Darkhei* — ways — is the whole road a life is on, the pattern you live out when nobody's grading you for it. And pleasing God here isn't a vague feeling; *ratsoth* is the word used for a sacrifice God actually accepts, delights in. So the verse is saying: when the whole direction of how you live becomes something God takes real pleasure in, something happens that you didn't engineer — He turns your actual enemies, hostile people genuinely working against you, into people at peace with you.

That's not a negotiating tactic. It's a promise about where real peace with people actually comes from. You can spend your energy trying to manage the enemy, or you can spend it on your own walk and let God handle what you can't. The second one is harder to see working in the moment, and it's the one that actually works.
