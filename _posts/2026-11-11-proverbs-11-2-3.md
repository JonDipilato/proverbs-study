Pride never shows up by itself. Verse 2 says when it comes, shame comes right behind it, like the two are tied together with a rope. You can't have one without eventually getting the other. Maybe not today, maybe not this week, but it's coming. Meanwhile the person who stays low, who doesn't need to win every argument or be the smartest guy in the room, ends up with something better: wisdom.

Verse 3 picks up the same idea from a different angle. Integrity — being the same person whether anyone's watching or not — is what guides you. It's not flashy. It doesn't win arguments or get you noticed. It just quietly keeps you pointed the right direction, the way a guardrail keeps a car on the road without anyone thanking it. Crookedness does the opposite. It might get you ahead for a minute, but it destroys the very people who use it, because a bent path always eventually breaks.

Here's where it gets personal: pride and crookedness usually travel together. The guy who can't admit he's wrong is often the same guy who'll bend the truth a little to avoid looking bad. The fix for both is the same unglamorous thing — staying low enough to be corrected, and straight enough that you don't need to hide anything. That's not weakness. That's the only thing strong enough to actually hold up over time.

You won't feel wisdom or integrity working today. You'll just notice, months from now, that you're still standing while the shortcuts you didn't take would've buried you.
