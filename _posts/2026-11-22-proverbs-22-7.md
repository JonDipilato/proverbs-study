Verse 7 opens with the obvious half — rich rules, poor doesn't, everybody already knows that. The sharp edge is the second half. Solomon isn't talking about rich and poor anymore. He's talking about you, right now, and whoever is holding your debt.

*Loveh* and *malveh* — borrower and lender — are the same word in Hebrew, just worn two different ways. One man ties the rope, the other gets tied by it. That's what borrowing actually is: not free money, a leash. And the man on the other end of that leash isn't your employee or your partner. Solomon calls him your master, and calls you *eved* — a slave. Not a bad metaphor. The actual word for owned.

Nobody feels like a slave swiping a card. That's exactly how it gets you — a little bit at a time, no chains you can see, until one day every paycheck has somewhere else to go before it's yours. The fix isn't complicated, it's just hard: stop handing people the rope. Pay down what's already tied to you, and quit picking up new line.
