Nobody thinks they're the villain. That's the whole problem verse 2 is pointing at. Every man's *derek* looks *yashar* to him — straight, justified, fine — because he's grading his own homework. The guy who cuts corners at work has a reason. The guy who snapped at his wife has a reason. The guy who's cheating on his taxes has a reason. Solomon isn't saying those men are stupid. He's saying the LORD isn't looking at how it looks to you — He's weighing your *libboth*, the real motive sitting underneath the reason you gave yourself.

Verse 3 is the fix, and it's blunt: actually doing *tsedaqah* and *mishpat* — real right-dealing, a real fair call — beats *zebach*, sacrifice, every time. That's not just an Old Testament ritual thing. It's every version of "I went to church," "I said my prayers," "I meant well" that a man uses to feel settled with God while the actual account — what he did to his family, his coworkers, his word — stays unpaid. God's not impressed by the ritual. He's checking the ledger. Today, don't just ask if your way feels right. Ask if it is.
